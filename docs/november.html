November Festivals of India

November is a month of light, gratitude, harvest celebration, family bonding, and spiritual reflection. Festivals emphasize prosperity, discipline, and social responsibility.

Diwali (Deepavali)

Region

Pan-India

Historical Origin

Diwali has multiple regional origins, including the return of Lord Rama to Ayodhya after defeating Ravana and the worship of Goddess Lakshmi, symbol of prosperity and well-being.

Why This Festival Exists

It marks the victory of light over darkness and encourages renewal, cleanliness, and ethical living after the monsoon season.

How It Is Celebrated

Homes are cleaned, lamps are lit, prayers are offered, fireworks are used, and families share meals and gifts.

Traditional Food Knowledge

Sweets made from ghee, nuts, and jaggery restore energy and warmth as winter approaches.

Traditional Clothing

New traditional attire in bright colors symbolizes renewal and joy.

Values Passed On

Hope, prosperity, generosity, inner illumination.

Govardhan Puja (Annakut)

Region

North India, Gujarat

Historical Origin

Govardhan Puja commemorates Lord Krishna lifting Govardhan Hill to protect villagers from floods, emphasizing harmony with nature.

Why This Festival Exists

It teaches gratitude toward land, food, and natural resources, especially after harvest season.

How It Is Celebrated

A mountain of food (Annakut) is prepared and offered, followed by community feasts.

Traditional Food Knowledge

Multiple vegetable and grain dishes reflect agricultural abundance.

Traditional Clothing

Festive regional attire reflecting prosperity.

Values Passed On

Environmental respect, humility, gratitude.

Chhath Puja

Region

Bihar, Jharkhand, Uttar Pradesh

Historical Origin

Chhath Puja is one of the oldest Vedic festivals dedicated to the Sun God (Surya), thanking him for life, health, and sustenance.

Why This Festival Exists

It promotes solar worship, cleanliness of water bodies, and disciplined living.

How It Is Celebrated

Devotees observe rigorous fasts, offer prayers to the rising and setting sun, and perform rituals at riverbanks.

Traditional Food Knowledge

Thekua and seasonal fruits provide nourishment without excessive spices or fats.

Traditional Clothing

Traditional sarees and dhotis suitable for early winter mornings.

Values Passed On

Discipline, gratitude, environmental purity.
