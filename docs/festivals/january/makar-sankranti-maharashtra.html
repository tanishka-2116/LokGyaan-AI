About the Festival

Makar Sankranti is celebrated in Maharashtra during mid-January and marks the Sun’s transition into the zodiac sign Capricorn. It represents the end of winter and the beginning of longer, warmer days.

How Makar Sankranti is Celebrated

Families visit relatives and neighbors to exchange sweets and good wishes. People greet each other with the phrase “Tilgul ghya, god god bola”, meaning “Accept this sweet and speak sweet words.”

Homes are decorated, community gatherings are held, and cultural programs often take place.

Traditional Food

Food prepared during Makar Sankranti is based on ingredients that provide warmth and energy during winter.

  • Tilgul: Sesame seeds and jaggery laddoos
  • Puran Poli: Sweet flatbread

Sesame and jaggery are rich in nutrients and help maintain body heat during cold weather.

Traditional Clothing

In Maharashtra, women traditionally wear black sarees with colorful borders. Black is believed to absorb warmth during winter.

Men usually wear traditional dhoti-kurta or simple ethnic attire.

Rituals & Cultural Practices

  • Exchange of Tilgul as a symbol of harmony
  • Visiting elders and relatives
  • Community prayers and cultural gatherings

Traditional Knowledge & Seasonal Wisdom

Makar Sankranti reflects ancient knowledge of solar movement, seasonal cycles, and nutrition. The festival teaches balance, positive communication, and living in harmony with nature.

Visual Culture

The festival is visually identified by sesame sweets, colorful sarees, decorated homes, and warm community interactions.
