January Festivals of India

Makar Sankranti

Makar Sankranti marks the Sun’s northward journey and the end of winter. In Maharashtra, people exchange Tilgul and wish sweetness in relationships.

Region: Maharashtra

Food: Tilgul, puran poli

Pongal

Pongal is a four-day harvest festival of Tamil Nadu that thanks the Sun, farmers, cattle, and nature for abundance.

Region: Tamil Nadu

Food: Sweet Pongal, Ven Pongal

Lohri

Lohri is a winter harvest festival celebrated around community bonfires in Punjab and Haryana, symbolizing warmth and togetherness.

Region: Punjab, Haryana

Food: Revri, peanuts, popcorn

Magh Bihu (Bhogali Bihu)

Magh Bihu celebrates the end of the harvest season in Assam with community feasts and traditional rice-based food.

Region: Assam

Food: Pitha, laru

Uttarayan

Uttarayan is famous for kite flying in Gujarat and marks the Sun’s northward movement with joy and celebration.

Region: Gujarat

Food: Undhiyu, chikki

Paush Purnima

Paush Purnima is a Hindu spiritual observance marking the full moon of the Paush month. Devotees take holy river baths, perform charity, and begin sacred vows such as the Kalpavas.

Region: North India

Practice: River bathing, fasting, donation

Vaikuntha Ekadashi

Vaikuntha Ekadashi is an important Vaishnav festival dedicated to Lord Vishnu. Devotees observe fasting and visit temples, believing the gates of Vaikuntha are open on this day.

Region: South & North India

Practice: Fasting, night vigil, temple worship

Tusu Parab

Tusu Parab is a tribal harvest festival celebrated in Jharkhand, West Bengal, and Odisha. It involves folk songs, dances, and offerings to the goddess Tusu for agricultural prosperity.

Region: Jharkhand, West Bengal

Practice: Folk rituals, singing, community worship

Republic Day

Republic Day commemorates the adoption of the Constitution of India on 26 January 1950. It is celebrated with national pride, parades, and cultural performances across the country.

Region: Pan-India

Practice: Parades, flag hoisting, cultural programs
