April Festivals of India

April marks regional New Years, harvest gratitude, and the full arrival of spring. Festivals during this month align society with agricultural cycles, renewal, and disciplined beginnings.

Baisakhi

Region

Punjab, Haryana

Historical Origin

Baisakhi began as a harvest festival celebrating the ripening of rabi crops. In 1699, it also became historically significant with the formation of the Khalsa by Guru Gobind Singh.

Why This Festival Exists

It acknowledges agricultural abundance and reinforces courage, equality, and collective responsibility in society.

How It Is Celebrated

Farmers thank the land, people visit gurudwaras, participate in Nagar Kirtans, and perform Bhangra and Gidda.

Traditional Food Knowledge

Wheat-based foods like roti and kheer reflect the fresh harvest and provide strength for farm work.

Traditional Clothing

Bright-colored Punjabi attire symbolizes joy and prosperity.

Values Passed On

Bravery, gratitude, equality, community service.

Vishu

Region

Kerala

Historical Origin

Vishu marks the solar New Year and has roots in ancient astronomical observations used to align human life with cosmic cycles.

Why This Festival Exists

It teaches the importance of beginnings — what one sees first (Vishukkani) sets mental intention for the year.

How It Is Celebrated

Families view the Vishukkani arrangement at dawn, burst firecrackers, and elders gift Vishukkaineettam (coins).

Traditional Food Knowledge

Vishu Sadya uses seasonal vegetables that aid digestion during rising temperatures.

Traditional Clothing

Traditional kasavu sarees and mundu made of breathable cotton.

Values Passed On

Positive thinking, discipline, gratitude.

Puthandu (Tamil New Year)

Region

Tamil Nadu

Historical Origin

Puthandu follows ancient Tamil solar calendars that emphasize balance between nature and society.

Why This Festival Exists

It provides a structured mental reset for the year and aligns personal conduct with dharmic principles.

How It Is Celebrated

Homes are decorated with kolams, Panchangam is read, and families share festive meals.

Traditional Food Knowledge

Mango pachadi combines sweet, sour, bitter, and spicy tastes, teaching acceptance of life’s dualities.

Traditional Clothing

Fresh cotton and silk attire in light spring colors.

Values Passed On

Wisdom, balance, acceptance.

Bohag Bihu (Rongali Bihu)

Region

Assam

Historical Origin

Bohag Bihu marks the Assamese New Year and the beginning of the agricultural season.

Why This Festival Exists

It renews human-nature relationships before farming begins.

How It Is Celebrated

Traditional Bihu dances, music, cattle worship, and community feasts are held.

Traditional Food Knowledge

Rice cakes like pitha provide energy for upcoming agricultural labor.

Traditional Clothing

Mekhela chador and dhoti in white with red patterns.

Values Passed On

Joy, respect for nature, cultural continuity.
